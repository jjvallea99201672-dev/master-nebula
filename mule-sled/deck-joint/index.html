MULE Bridge — Deck Joint

Config C sled (6.6 m deck, 2.4 m wide) · Gap plate 1.2 m · Concept drawing, not fabrication-ready · 2026-11-18

Read this first: the plate is pinned at one end only. The far end rides in a slotted bearing (±50 mm slide). A plate pinned rigidly at both ends would bind the moment two sleds move relative to each other — and they always do, because each sled holds station independently in wind. Station-keeping tolerance is ±50 mm; beyond that, stop crossings.

Notes

  • Load path: car on the plate → plate → fixed pin (left) + bearing pad (right) → two sled decks. Each pin takes ~half a car in shear (~9 kN); a Ø30 mm stainless pin is far inside its rating. Pins are wear items, not strength items.
  • Why the slot: each sled holds station independently. In wind they never sit perfectly still relative to each other — the gap breathes by millimetres and the decks pitch by a degree or two. The slot absorbs it. Without it, the joint binds and the plate becomes a lever trying to tip a sled.
  • Tolerances: station-keeping ±50 mm (slot travel). Gap opens beyond that → stop crossings, re-station the sleds. The plate stays pinned at the fixed end if it ever disengages — it hangs, it doesn't fall in the river.
  • Inspection: pins and slots are the wear points — check for elongation and corrosion after every deployment; grease the housings. The plate is the cheapest part on the bridge; replace it rather than repairing it.
